⚡ METRIC THRESHOLD — RETRACTION: BTC DID NOT LOSE ITS 200-DAY MOVING AVERAGE

Yesterday's brief flagged "BTC crossing below the 200-day MA on volume" as a critical development, citing a 200-day SMA of $78,440.38 from the Investing.com Bitcoin Real-Time (BRTI) technical panel. That was wrong, and the error is mechanical rather than a matter of judgement. A 200-day simple moving average can only move by (new bar − dropped bar) ÷ 200 per day — a few dollars. The same panel today reads $77,553.81, an $887 move in twenty-four hours, and its "MA50" moved $1,506 over the same span. Both are impossible for daily-bar averages, which means the BRTI panel is computing on an intraday period, not on days.

Two independent live prints give the real number: Newhedge's Mayer Multiple of 1.08 — a ratio defined as price ÷ the 200-day MA — implies a 200-day of ~$70,533 at a $76,176 spot, and BGeometrics separately publishes a 200-day SMA of ~$69,434. Both put BTC comfortably above its 200-day average, by roughly 8–10%. The framework's critical criterion was never met. Lens 5 corrected 🔴 → 🟡.

🟡 1. HALVING / CYCLE — fresh, full weight

~29 months post-halving. Miner economics softened materially in twenty-four hours: hashrate 880.65 EH/s (−14.72%), miner revenue $33.31M/day (−13.95%), Puell Multiple 0.93 (−13.80%) — while network difficulty held unchanged at 127.45T. Read that combination carefully. Difficulty adjusts to hashrate with a lag, so an unchanged difficulty alongside a 14.7% one-day hashrate drop points at block-time variance in the estimator rather than at machines being switched off; a genuine 15% capitulation would show up in the next difficulty adjustment, not instantly. Puell below 1.0 means miner revenue is running under its yearly average, which is historically a value-zone reading as often as it is a stress reading. Worth watching, not yet worth downgrading.
